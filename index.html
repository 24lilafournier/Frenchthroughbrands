Learning a language also means discovering the society it is spoken in.

That starts with the small things you'd come across anyway! A shop window, an ad, a café menu, a museum or a news story can all become a way into French as it is actually used today. Instead of learning vocabulary and grammar in isolation, you meet them in situations that feel familiar and useful. So when you need French outside the lesson — to order, ask a question, talk to someone or deal with an everyday situation — you have already seen the language in context.

Lessons

What you’ll work on

Whether you are starting from scratch or already speak French, each lesson starts from what you need to be able to do in French. The progression follows the CEFR (Common European Framework of Reference for Languages), so what you work on at each level matches recognized international standards — useful if you need your level for a job, a school application or an official test.

If you’re starting out, the aim is to give you French you can use quickly: understand essential exchanges, ask for what you need, answer, find your way around and build your first sentences without waiting until you “know all the grammar”.

As you progress, you become familiar with the complexities that make French difficult to use spontaneously: tenses, articles, agreement, pronouns and turns of phrase that make sense on paper but are still harder to reach for when you speak.

At an advanced level, the work becomes more nuanced: choosing more natural phrasing, expressing finer distinctions, becoming more precise and understanding more clearly how French is actually used.

Each lesson is designed around you, using brands, situations and content carefully chosen for your level, your goals and what genuinely interests you. The exercises and role-plays are then created around that selection, so the French we work on is directly useful to you.

In practice

During the lesson

Each lesson follows a simple thread: review, explore, understand, practise and reuse.

We start with a warm-up.
A few minutes to revisit what we covered previously, get back into French and see what still needs reinforcement.

Then we move into a topic and work on the language through it.
A brand, a place, an ad, an article, a video or a real-life situation provides the thread. As you observe, listen, read or discuss, you encounter vocabulary, grammatical structures, more natural phrasing and pronunciation patterns that help you talk about the topic.

You put them into practice straight away.
Short exercises help you understand how the language works, use it several times and correct what still feels uncertain, without leaving the context of the lesson.

You then reuse what you have just worked on in more open-ended activities.
Depending on the lesson, this might involve one or more role-plays, a guided conversation, a task to complete or a more spontaneous exchange. The aim is to give you several chances to use the vocabulary, structures and phrasing we have worked on, with less and less support.

By the end of the lesson, the aim is not only for you to have understood something new, but to have already used it several times.

After the lesson

The lesson does not stop when our time is up.

I send you a lesson recap with what we worked on, the words and expressions that came up, and the key things to remember.

It also helps me prepare what comes next, so the following lesson does not start from scratch.

Recap Georgina · Lesson n°4 — 13 août 2026 Level A1
French articles : countable & uncountable nouns
Vocabulary 1
une maison — a house / home. Maison is feminine: une maison, la maison. It can mean the place where people live, but also a fashion house: une maison de couture. Maison is countable.
Vocabulary 2
de la couture — sewing / dressmaking. Couture is feminine: la couture. Here, it refers to the activity in general, so it is uncountable and we use de la: de la couture.
One thing to remember
French nouns ending in -ure or -ion are often feminine: la couture, la culture, la situation, la collection.

Where are you with your French right now?

You’re new to French

Start speaking from your first lessons, understand simple exchanges and gradually build the foundations you need to go further.

Find your words more easily, hesitate less and make better use of what you already know when speaking.

Make fewer mistakes, build more accurate sentences and express exactly what you mean without losing spontaneity.

Go beyond textbook French, find more idiomatic ways to express yourself and better understand the nuances of the language as it is actually used.

A trip, a job interview, an exam, a new role or a move to France: lessons can also be built around the situations in which you will actually need French.

About

I’m Lila, a French teacher with a Master’s degree in Law, Economics and Management from Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne. Before teaching, I worked in finance across Europe and North America. I’m also currently learning Thai, so I know the difference between knowing a rule and being able to use is when you actually need it! And that shapes how I teach grammar, vocabulary and pronunciation : always grounded in real context, not as isolated rules.

I called these lessons French through brands because I like to start from things that already exist around us. A brand is never just a logo: it can tell you about a habit, an era, a profession, the way people consume or part of French history. And it immediately gives us something to observe, understand and talk about.

That does not mean every lesson is about fashion or luxury. We might start from a sports retailer, a café, a city, a work of art, your professional field or a current topic. What matters is that French is never left on the page by itself.

I learn languages too, so I know the difference between knowing a rule and being able to use it when you actually need it. That is also why we work on grammar, vocabulary and pronunciation exactly when they become useful in what you are trying to say.
